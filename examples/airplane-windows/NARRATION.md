# Narration — Why Airplane Windows Are Round

Each row is the exact transcript of one shot. The JSON is the render input.

| Shot | Narration |
| --- | --- |
| hook | Two window shapes. One concentrates more stress. Look at the corners. Which would you trust? |
| climb | At cruising altitude, the outside air is thin. The cabin holds air at higher pressure. |
| cabin | That difference pushes outward on the fuselage. The aircraft skin carries this load around the windows. |
| pressure-section | In cross section, air pushes outward in every direction. The shell stretches like a balloon. |
| cycles | The pressure difference rises during a climb and falls on descent. Every flight repeats the load. |
| load-path | Cut a hole in that skin and forces must travel through the metal around the opening. |
| corner | A sharp corner forces an abrupt turn. The local stress can spike right at that corner. |
| curves | A wider curve eases that turn. Stress still concentrates, but rounding reduces the peak. |
| fatigue | Repeated loads can grow a small crack, a little at a time. Engineers call this fatigue. |
| comet | In the nineteen fifties, Comet jet crashes prompted an investigation into fatigue in the pressure cabin. |
| water-test | Investigators pressurized a test fuselage in a water tank and watched cracks grow across repeated cycles. |
| antenna | In one crash, fatigue evidence pointed to a radio antenna opening. Fastener details mattered too. |
| reinforcement | Engineers also reinforce the frame and test for fatigue. The window shape is part of a system. |
| return-window | Back at your seat, the window might look oval or rectangular. Look for the smoothly rounded corners. |
| closing | Those curves reduce local stress. That is why the corners are built for flight after flight. |
